- **Lamp Spectrum & Aperture Concentration:** A $100–125 W$ incandescent heat-lamp tungsten filament ($T_{\mathrm{fil}} \approx 2300–2450 K$) peaks at $\lambda_{\max} = 2897.8 µm K / 2400 K = \mathbf{1.21 µm}$ (near-IR) and emits $\approx 82\%\text{--}86\%$ of its electrical power as radiation. Funneling the full allowed $29.6 cm \times 29.6 cm$ top aperture ($A_{\mathrm{ap}} = 876 cm^2$) down onto the $7.5 cm$ mouth of the 9 oz clear plastic cup ($A_{\mathrm{cup}} = 44.2 cm^2$) via a specular shiny-side aluminum foil compound reflector ($\rho_{\mathrm{NIR,spec}} = 0.88\text{--}0.91$ shiny side vs. $0.58\text{--}0.68$ dull side!) provides a geometric concentration potential of $C_{\mathrm{geom}} = 876 / 44.2 = \mathbf{19.8\times}$  **[S12,S19]**.
- **Near-IR Water Surface Absorption & Stable Stratification (`Clarifications #1–#4`):** From Hale & Querry (1973) and Kou et al. (1993)  **[S19,S20]**, liquid water's absorption coefficient jumps from $\mu_a(1.0 µm) = 0.36 /cm$ to $\mu_a(1.45 µm) = 28.8 /cm$ ($\delta_p = 0.35 mm$) and $\mu_a(1.95 µm) = 114 /cm$ ($\delta_p = 0.088 mm$). Thus **$>68\%$ of absorbed lamp power is deposited directly into the top $10 mm$ water layer**. Because warmer water above $4 °C$ is less dense than cooler water below it ($\partial\rho/\partial z > 0, \mathrm{Ra} < 0$), natural convection is completely suppressed! Under `Clarifications #1–#4` (zero stirring/swirling; ES measures top water layer near cup center)  **[S02]**, this stratified top layer ($C_{\mathrm{top,eff}} \approx 215 J/K$ vs. bulk $418.4 J/K$) boosts measured $\mathrm{TG}$ by **$1.5\times\text{--}1.9\times$** over a mixed cup.
- **Why Non-Attached Glazing (`Clarification #5`) Saves $3.5–7.0 °C$:** As proved in Lienhard Example 11.11 (pp. 662–664)  **[S12]**, evaporative mass transfer from an uncovered $40 °C$ water cup ($\dot{Q}_{\mathrm{evap}} \approx 2.22 W$, $L_v = 2406 kJ/kg$) accounts for **$73\%\text{--}82\%$ of all top-surface heat loss** and cools the exact top skin the ES thermometer measures! Resting a non-attached clear BoPET film window ($\tau_{\mathrm{NIR}} = 0.90$, no tape/adhesives/clips on the cup per `Clarification #5`) traps saturated $100\%\ \mathrm{RH}$ air above the cup and eliminates evaporative loss.

}### Worked Example — Rapid Cram Drill 1: Eight-Scale Temperature Crossovers `[Rule 5.c.i]`

Prove algebraically the exact temperatures where **(a)** Kelvin and Fahrenheit read the same number ($T_{\mathrm{K}} = T_{\mathrm{F}}$), and **(b)** Celsius and Delisle read the same number ($T_{\mathrm{C}} = T_{\mathrm{De}}$)  **[S25,S27]**.

**Solution.**
**(a)** Substitute $T_{\mathrm{C}} = T_{\mathrm{K}} - 273.15$ into $T_{\mathrm{F}} = 1.8\,T_{\mathrm{C}} + 32$ and set $T_{\mathrm{K}} = T_{\mathrm{F}} = x$: $x = 1.8(x - 273.15) + 32 \implies 0.8x = 459.67 \implies x = \mathbf{574.5875\text{ K} = 574.5875\ ^\circ\mathrm{F}}$.
**(b)** Set $T_{\mathrm{C}} = T_{\mathrm{De}} = y$ in $T_{\mathrm{De}} = 150 - 1.5\,T_{\mathrm{C}}$: $y = 150 - 1.5y \implies 2.5y = 150 \implies y = \mathbf{+60.0\ ^\circ\mathrm{C} = +60.0\ ^\circ\mathrm{De}}$.

**✓ Verified by:** Independent Python script `check_sec21_23.py` (`SEC23_SCALE_INTERSECTIONS`): $x = 574.5875$, $y = 60.0$.

### Worked Example — Rapid Cram Drill 2: Open-Cup Evaporation Loss vs. BoPET Glazing Cap `[Clarification #5]`

During a 10-minute 2027 collector run, an uncovered 9 oz cup holding 100 mL ($100.0 g$) of water at mean surface temperature $40.0 °C$ ($L_v = 2406 J/g$) loses $\Delta m_{\mathrm{evap}} = 0.550 g$ of water by evaporation  **[S12]**. How many degrees Celsius of temperature gain $\mathrm{TG}$ are lost from **(a)** the ES-measured top stratified layer ($C_{\mathrm{top,eff}} = 215.0 J/K$), and **(b)** the bulk water ($C_{\mathrm{bulk}} = 418.4 J/K$)?

**Solution.**
The latent heat removed directly from the water surface skin by evaporating $0.550 g$ is $Q_{\mathrm{evap}} = \Delta m_{\mathrm{evap}}\,L_v = (0.550 g)(2406 J/g) = \mathbf{1323.3 J}$.
**(a) Top stratified layer loss:** $\Delta T_{\mathrm{top,lost}} = Q_{\mathrm{evap}}/C_{\mathrm{top,eff}} = 1323.3 J / 215.0 J/K = \mathbf{6.155 °C}$ (**6.2 °C saved by a non-attached BoPET cap!**).
**(b) Bulk-averaged loss:** $\Delta T_{\mathrm{bulk,lost}} = Q_{\mathrm{evap}}/C_{\mathrm{bulk}} = 1323.3 J / 418.4 J/K = \mathbf{3.163 °C}$.

**✓ Verified by:** Independent Python script `check_sec21_23.py` (`SEC23_EVAPORATION_PENALTY_DRILL`): $Q_{\mathrm{evap}} = 1323.3 J$, $\Delta T_{\mathrm{top}} = 6.155 °C$.

### Worked Example — Rapid Cram Drill 3: Rankine First-Law vs. Second-Law Exergy Efficiency `[Cornell 2027 E1 Q29]`

In Cornell 2027 (`E1 Q29`)  **[S25]**, a Rankine steam cycle absorbs $q_{\mathrm{in}} = 2257.19 kJ/kg$ in a boiler heated by a furnace at $T_{\mathrm{source}} = 600 K$ (ambient dead state $T_0 = 300 K$) and produces net turbine work $w_{\mathrm{net}} = 109.92 kJ/kg$. Compute **(a)** the First-Law thermal efficiency $\eta_1$, **(b)** the supplied exergy $X_{\mathrm{in}}$, and **(c)** the Second-Law exergy efficiency $\eta_2$ (`TB #8`).

**Solution.**
**(a)** First-Law efficiency: $\eta_1 = w_{\mathrm{net}}/q_{\mathrm{in}} = 109.92 / 2257.19 = 0.048698 = \mathbf{4.870\%}$.
**(b)** Maximum reversible (Carnot) work extractable from $q_{\mathrm{in}}$ between $600 K$ and $300 K$ ($\eta_C = 1 - 300/600 = 0.5000$): $X_{\mathrm{in}} = q_{\mathrm{in}}(1 - T_0/T_{\mathrm{source}}) = 2257.19(0.5000) = \mathbf{1128.60 kJ/kg}$.
**(c)** Second-Law efficiency: $\eta_2 = w_{\mathrm{net}}/X_{\mathrm{in}} = \eta_1/\eta_C = 4.8698\% / 50.00\% = \mathbf{9.740\%}$.

**✓ Verified by:** Independent Python script `check_sec21_23.py` (`SEC23_EXERGY_SECOND_LAW_EFF`): $\eta_1 = 4.870\%$, $X_{\mathrm{in}} = 1128.6 kJ/kg$, $\eta_2 = 9.740\%$.
