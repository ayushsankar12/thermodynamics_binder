### [TRAP & DIAGNOSTIC: Six Exact Temperature-Scale Intersections & Interval vs. Point-Temperature Trap { **[S07,S13]**}]

**Exact Crossovers:**
(**1**) $^\circ\mathrm{C} = {^\circ\mathrm{F}}$ at $\mathbf{-40^\circ}$ ($233.15 K$);
(**2**) $\mathrm{K} = {^\circ\mathrm{F}}$ at $\mathbf{+574.5875}$ ($301.4375 °C$);
(**3**) $^\circ\mathrm{C} = {^\circ\mathrm{De}}$ at $\mathbf{+60^\circ}$ ($333.15 K$);
(**4**) $^\circ\mathrm{F} = {^\circ\mathrm{De}}$ at $\mathbf{+96.3636^\circ}$ ($1060/11^\circ$, where $T_C = 1180/33 = 35.7576 °C$);
(**5**) $^\circ\mathrm{C} = {^\circ}\text{Rø}$ at $\mathbf{+15.7895^\circ}$ ($+300/19^\circ$, $288.9395 K$);
(**6**) $^\circ\mathrm{C} = {^\circ}\text{Ré} = {^\circ\mathrm{N}}$ at $\mathbf{0^\circ}$ ($273.15 K$).
**Interval Trap:** A temperature *difference* converts without the additive offset: $\Delta T[\mathrm{K}] = \Delta T[^\circ\mathrm{C}] = \tfrac{5}{9}\Delta T[^\circ\mathrm{F}] = \tfrac{5}{9}\Delta T[^\circ\mathrm{R}]$. Therefore $c_w = 4184 J/kg/K \equiv 4184 J/kg/°C$ (never add $273.15$ to a denominator $\text{K}^{-1}$!).

### Worked Example — Glass Flask Overflow & Pendulum Drift `[T2 $\cdot$ 60 s]`

A Pyrex flask ($V_0 = 500.0 mL$, $\alpha_g = 3.2×10^{-6} /K$) filled to the brim with mercury ($\beta_{\mathrm{Hg}} = 181×10^{-6} /K$) is heated from $20.0 °C$ to $80.0 °C$ ($\Delta T = 60.0 K$). Find the mercury overflow $\Delta V_{\mathrm{over}}$.
**Sol.:** $\beta_{\mathrm{app}} = \beta_{\mathrm{Hg}} - 3\alpha_g = (181 - 9.6)\times 10^{-6} = 171.4×10^{-6} /K$.
$\Delta V_{\mathrm{over}} = \beta_{\mathrm{app}} V_0 \Delta T = (171.4×10^{-6})(500.0 mL)(60.0) = 5.14 mL$.

**✓ Verified by:** Exact $V_{\mathrm{Hg}} - V_{\mathrm{flask}} = 500(1.01086 - 1.000576) = 5.142 mL$.

### [TRAP & DIAGNOSTIC: Four Classic Thermal-Expansion Traps { **[S13,S35]**}]

**1. Hole / Cavity Trap:** A hole or cavity in a uniform solid expands *outward* at the exact same rate ($\alpha$ or $3\alpha$) as a solid plug of the same material.\\
**2. Water $0\text{--}4\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ Contraction:** Water *contracts* ($\beta < 0$) when warmed from $0 °C$ ($\rho = 999.84 kg/m^3$) to $3.984 °C$ ($\rho_{\max} = 999.97 kg/m^3$), then expands for $T > 4 °C$.\\
**3. Bimetallic Bend Direction:** When heated ($\Delta T > 0$), a bimetallic strip bends *toward* the lower-$\alpha$ metal (higher-$\alpha$ metal on the outside convex arc); when cooled ($\Delta T < 0$), it bends toward the higher-$\alpha$ metal.\\
**4. Ruler Scale Trap:** A metal ruler heated above its calibration temp $T_0$ expands, so its tick marks spread apart and it *under-reads* true length: $L_{\mathrm{true}} = L_{\mathrm{read}}[1 + \alpha_{\mathrm{ruler}}(T - T_0)]$.

### [FORMULA & DECISION CARD: Deterministic 5-Zone Ice ($T_{\mathrm{ice}} \le 0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$) + Liquid Water ($0\le T_w \le 100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$) + Steam ($T_s \ge 100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$) Algorithm { **[S09,S13]**}]

**Reference Benchmarks ($1 atm$):** $c_{\mathrm{ice}} = 2093 J/kg/K$, $c_w = 4184 J/kg/K$, $c_{\mathrm{steam},P} = 2080 J/kg/K$, $L_f = 333.4 kJ/kg$ (OpenStax $334 kJ/kg$), $L_v = 2256.4 kJ/kg$.\\
**Step 1 — Compute Energy Benchmarks Relative to $0 °C$ Liquid Water ($\Delta H_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0$ at $0 °C$ liquid):**
- Heat needed to warm subcooled ice to $0 °C$: $Q_1 = m_{\mathrm{ice}} c_{\mathrm{ice}}(0 - T_{\mathrm{ice}})$. \quad Heat needed to melt all ice at $0 °C$: $Q_2 = m_{\mathrm{ice}} L_f$.
- Heat needed to warm all melted ice + initial water to $100 °C$: $Q_3 = m_{\mathrm{ice}} c_w(100) + m_w c_w(100 - T_w)$.
- Heat released if warm water cools to $0 °C$: $Q_{w\to 0} = m_w c_w(T_w - 0)$. \quad Heat released if steam cools to $100 °C$ and condenses: $Q_{s\to 100,\ell} = m_s c_s(T_s - 100) + m_s L_v$.
- Total heat released if steam cools all the way to $0 °C$ liquid water: $Q_{s\to 0,\ell} = Q_{s\to 100,\ell} + m_s c_w(100)$. \quad Let $Q_{\mathrm{avail},0} = Q_{w\to 0} + Q_{s\to 0,\ell}$.

**Step 2 — Locate Unique Equilibrium Zone:**{

| **Condition** | **Final Phase State** | **Exact Algebraic Equation for Final Temperature $T_f$ or Phase Fraction** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Zone 1:** $Q_{\mathrm{avail},0} < Q_1$ | **100% Solid Ice** ($T_f < 0 °C$) | $T_f = -\dfrac{Q_1 - Q_{\mathrm{avail},0}}{(m_{\mathrm{ice}} + m_w + m_s)c_{\mathrm{ice}}}\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$ (all water/steam freezes and subcools) |
| **Zone 2:** $Q_1 \le Q_{\mathrm{avail},0} \le Q_1 + Q_2$ | **Ice–Water Slush at $T_f = 0.0 °C$** | Mass of ice melted: $m_{\mathrm{melted}} = \dfrac{Q_{\mathrm{avail},0} - Q_1}{L_f}$; remaining ice $= m_{\mathrm{ice}} - m_{\mathrm{melted}}$ |
| **Zone 3:** $Q_1+Q_2 < Q_{\mathrm{avail},0} < Q_1+Q_2+(m_{\mathrm{ice}}+m_w+m_s)c_w(100)$ | **100% Liquid Water** ($0 °C < T_f < 100 °C$) | $T_f = \dfrac{Q_{\mathrm{avail},0} - (Q_1 + Q_2)}{(m_{\mathrm{ice}} + m_w + m_s)c_w}\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$ |
| **Zone 4:** $0 \le Q_{s\to 100,\ell} - (Q_1+Q_2+Q_3) \le (m_{\mathrm{ice}}+m_w)L_v$ | **Water–Steam Mix at $T_f = 100.0 °C$** | Mass of steam condensed: $m_{\mathrm{cond}} = \dfrac{Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3 - m_s c_s(T_s-100)}{L_v}$ |
| **Zone 5:** $m_s c_s(T_s - 100) > Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3 + (m_{\mathrm{ice}}+m_w)L_v$ | **100% Superheated Steam** ($T_f > 100 °C$) | $T_f = 100 + \dfrac{m_s c_s(T_s-100) - [Q_1+Q_2+Q_3+(m_{\mathrm{ice}}+m_w)L_v]}{(m_{\mathrm{ice}}+m_w+m_s)c_s}\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$ |

### Worked Example — Three-Phase Ice + Warm Water + Steam Calorimeter Check `[T2 $\cdot$ 75 s]`

Mix $m_{\mathrm{ice}} = 50.0 g$ ice at $-20.0 °C$, $m_w = 200.0 g$ liquid water at $30.0 °C$, and $m_s = 10.0 g$ steam at $100.0 °C$ at $1 atm$ ($c_{\mathrm{ice}}=2.093 J/g/K, c_w=4.184 J/g/K, L_f=333.4 J/g, L_v=2256.4 J/g$). Find $T_f$.
**Sol.:** $Q_1 = 50(2.093)(20) = 2093 J$; $Q_2 = 50(333.4) = 16670 J$, so $Q_1+Q_2 = 18763 J$. Available heat to $0 °C$ liquid: $Q_{w\to 0} = 200(4.184)(30) = 25104 J$; $Q_{s\to 0,\ell} = 10(2256.4) + 10(4.184)(100) = 26748 J$, so $Q_{\mathrm{avail},0} = 51852 J > 18763 J$ (Zone 3: 100% liquid). Total mass $M = 260.0 g$:
$T_f = (51852 - 18763)/[260.0 \times 4.184] = 33089 J/(1087.84 J/K) = 30.42 °C$.

**✓ Verified by:** Direct energy balance at $30.417\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$: $Q_{\mathrm{gain,ice}} = 2093+16670+50(4.184)(30.417) = 25126 J$; $Q_{\mathrm{loss},w+s} = 25126 J$.

### [TRAP & DIAGNOSTIC: Slope Ratio & Irreversible Step-Pressure Trap]

**1. Slope Ratio on $P$–$V$ vs. $T$–$S$:**
On $P$–$V$, the adiabat is steeper than the isotherm by $\gamma$: $(\partial P/\partial V)_S = -\gamma P/V = \gamma(\partial P/\partial V)_T$.
On $T$–$S$, the isochore is steeper than the isobar by $\gamma$: $(\partial T/\partial S)_V = T/(nC_{V,m}) = \gamma(\partial T/\partial S)_P$.\\
**2. Irreversible Constant-$P_{\mathrm{ext}}$ Step:**
If an ideal gas expands against a *sudden constant external pressure* $P_{\mathrm{ext}} = P_2$, work is the rectangle $W_{\mathrm{by}} = P_2(V_2 - V_1)$, NOT $nRT\ln(V_2/V_1)$ and NOT $(P_1V_1-P_2V_2)/(\gamma-1)$! For an adiabatic step against $P_2$, solve $nC_{V,m}(T_2-T_1) = -P_2(V_2-V_1) = -nR P_2(T_2/P_2 - T_1/P_1)$.

### [TIER: STATE & NATIONALS (Rule 5.c.vi–vii): Third Law & Low-$T$ Heat Capacities { **[S01,S15]**}]

**Nernst–Planck Statement:** As $T \to 0\,K$, the entropy of any perfect crystal approaches zero ($S \to 0$) and $\Delta S \to 0$ for any isothermal process. Consequently, $T = 0 K$ is unattainable in a finite number of steps, and $C_V, C_P, \alpha_L, \beta \to 0$ as $T \to 0\,K$ ($C_{\mathrm{Debye}} = \tfrac{12\pi^4}{5}nR(T/\Theta_D)^3 \propto T^3$; metals add electronic $\gamma_e T$).

### Worked Example — Reversible Engine vs. Irreversible Heat Transfer between Finite Bodies `[T3 $\cdot$ 75 s]`

Two identical copper blocks ($C = m c = 1000 J/K$ each) start at $T_{1,i} = 400 K$ and $T_{2,i} = 200 K$. Compare (a) direct contact ($W = 0$) with (b) running a reversible Carnot engine between them until they reach a common final temperature.
**Sol.:** (a) Direct contact ($\sum Q = 0$): $T_f = \tfrac{1}{2}(400 + 200) = 300.0 K$, and $\Delta S_{\mathrm{univ}} = C\ln(300/400) + C\ln(300/200) = 1000\ln(90000/80000) = 1000\ln(9/8) = +117.78 J/K$.
(b) Reversible engine ($\Delta S_{\mathrm{univ}} = 0$): $C\ln(T_f'/T_{1,i}) + C\ln(T_f'/T_{2,i}) = 0 \implies T_f' = \sqrt{T_{1,i}T_{2,i}} = \sqrt{400\times 200} = 282.84 K$, producing maximum work $W_{\max} = C(T_{1,i} + T_{2,i} - 2T_f') = 1000(600 - 565.685) = 34.31 kJ$.

**✓ Verified by:** Gouy–Stodola check at $T_f'=282.84\,\text{K}$: $W_{\max} > 0$ because arithmetic mean $300\,\text{K} >$ geometric mean $282.84\,\text{K}$.

### [TRAP & DIAGNOSTIC: Clockwise vs. Counterclockwise Loop & Polygon Cycle $Q_H$ Separation Trap]

**1. Cycle Orientation:** On both $P$–$V$ and $T$–$S$ planes, a **clockwise** loop is a heat engine ($W_{\mathrm{net}} = \text{enclosed area} > 0$), while a **counterclockwise** loop is a refrigerator/heat pump ($W_{\mathrm{net}} < 0$, work input required).
**2. Polygon $P$–$V$ Cycle Trap:** For a rectangular or triangular cycle on $P$–$V$, compute $Q_{\mathrm{leg}} = \Delta U_{\mathrm{leg}} + W_{\mathrm{by,leg}}$ on *every individual leg* separately; $Q_H$ is the sum of ONLY the positive-$Q$ legs (never include a heat-rejection leg $Q_{\mathrm{leg}} < 0$ inside $Q_H$!).

### Worked Example — Fastener Thermal-Bridge Short-Circuit `[T2 $\cdot$ 60 s]`

An insulation panel ($A_{\mathrm{ins}} = 0.100 m^2$, $L = 20.0 mm$, $k_{\mathrm{ins}} = 0.040 W/m/K$) is pierced by four aluminum bolts (total area $A_{\mathrm{bolt}} = 1.00×10^{-4} m^2 = 1.00 cm^2$, $L = 20.0 mm$, $k_{\mathrm{Al}} = 237 W/m/K$). Find the percentage increase in conductive heat loss caused by the bolts.
**Sol.:** Parallel conductances $G = kA/L$:
$G_{\mathrm{ins}} = (0.040)(0.0999)/0.020 = 0.200 W/K$ ($R_{\mathrm{ins}} = 5.00 K/W$).
$G_{\mathrm{bolt}} = (237)(1.00×10^{-4})/0.020 = 1.185 W/K$ ($R_{\mathrm{bolt}} = 0.844 K/W$).
$G_{\mathrm{tot}} = 0.200 + 1.185 = 1.385 W/K$ ($R_{\mathrm{tot}} = 0.722 K/W$).
Even though the bolts occupy only $0.10\%$ of the area, they carry $85.6\%$ of the heat flow and increase total heat loss by $\mathbf{6.93\times}$ ($+593\%$)!

**✓ Verified by:** $G_{\mathrm{tot}}/G_{\mathrm{ins,0}} = 1.3848 / 0.2000 = 6.924\times$.

### Worked Example — Three-Point Extraction of $T_{\mathrm{ss}}$ and $\tau$ `[T3 $\cdot$ 60 s]`

In a practice lamp run, water temp is $T_1 = 22.0 °C$ at $t_1 = 0$, $T_2 = 32.0 °C$ at $t_2 = 5.0 min$, and $T_3 = 39.5 °C$ at $t_3 = 10.0 min$. Predict $T(15.0 min)$.
**Sol.:** Equal intervals $\Delta t = 5.0 min$: step gains are $\Delta_1 = 10.0 °C$ and $\Delta_2 = 7.5 °C$, so $e^{-\Delta t/\tau} = \Delta_2/\Delta_1 = 0.750$.
The third 5-min gain is $\Delta_3 = 7.5(0.750) = 5.625 °C \implies T(15.0 min) = 39.5 + 5.625 = \mathbf{45.1 °C}$ ($T_{\mathrm{ss}} = 62.0 °C$, $\tau = 17.38 min$).

**✓ Verified by:** $62.0 - 40.0(0.750)^3 = 45.125 °C$.

### [FORMULA & DECISION CARD: 3-Minute Setup Prediction Model & Official Scoring Formulas (**[2027-RULE]** 6.a–j) { **[S01,S04]**}]

**1. Day-Of Final Water Temperature Prediction Equation (in $^\circ\mathrm{C}$, rounded to $0.1 °C$):**

$$
T_{\mathrm{pred}}(h, t_{\mathrm{heat}}, P_{\mathrm{lamp}}, T_i, T_{\mathrm{room}}) = T_i + s_{\mathrm{strat}}\!\left[\frac{P_{\mathrm{abs}}(h, P_{\mathrm{lamp}})}{G_{\mathrm{loss}}} - (T_i - T_{\mathrm{room}})\right]\!\big(1 - e^{-t_{\mathrm{heat}}/\tau}\big) - \Delta T_{\mathrm{cool}}(t_{\mathrm{wait}}),\quad
  P_{\mathrm{abs}}(h) = P_{\mathrm{abs}}(h_{\mathrm{ref}})\!\left(\frac{P_{\mathrm{lamp}}}{P_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)\!\left(\frac{h_{\mathrm{ref}} - z_{\mathrm{eff}}}{h - z_{\mathrm{eff}}}\right)^2
$$

where $C_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 418.4 J/K + C_{\mathrm{cup}} \approx 435 J/K$, $\tau = C_{\mathrm{eff}}/G_{\mathrm{loss}} \approx 900–1400 s$, $s_{\mathrm{strat}} = \mathrm{TG}_{\mathrm{top}}/\mathrm{TG}_{\mathrm{bulk}} \approx 1.25\text{--}1.38$ (top-layer stratification factor), and $\Delta T_{\mathrm{cool}} \approx (0.012 °C/s)\,t_{\mathrm{wait}}$ is post-lamp cooling during cup removal.\\
