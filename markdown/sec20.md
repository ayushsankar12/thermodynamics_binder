# §20: Misconceptions, Classic Sign/Unit Traps & Wrong-Answer Diagnostic Catalog {#sec:20}

> **[SCOPE: REQUIRED | All 2027 Written Syllabus Clauses 5.c.i–vii & Part II Device Testing]**

Empirical analysis of official answer keys across the $N = 8$ Division C tournament corpus (`E1–E8`, §`sec:22`)  **[S25,S26,S27,S28,S29,S30,S31,S32]** and physics education research (Meltzer 2004; Loverude et al. 2002  **[S35]**) proves that over $70\%$ of lost calculation points stem from **16 recurring conceptual, sign, unit, and phase-boundary traps** rather than unfamiliar physics. Exam writers deliberately construct multiple-choice distractors and multi-part FRQ pitfalls around these exact traps. This chapter organizes all 16 trap families into a fast **Symptom $\to$ Likely Cause $\to$ 5-Second Diagnostic $\to$ Exact Fix** lookup catalog, followed by a numerical multiplier reverse-diagnostic table and six worked diagnostic case studies.

| **Trap ID & Domain** | **Wrong Move / Symptom** | **Likely Cause / Why It Fails** | **5-Second Diagnostic & Exact Fix** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **`ERR-01`: Heat ($Q$) vs. Temperature ($T$) vs. Internal Energy ($U$)** <br> (§`sec:03`, §`sec:07`) | $\bullet$ Saying “Object A contains more heat than B.” <br> $\bullet$ Assuming $\Delta T = 0 \implies Q = 0$. <br> $\bullet$ Assuming $Q = 0 \implies \Delta T = 0$. | Confusing *state functions* ($T$, internal energy $U$) with *boundary transit energy* ($Q$, path function). In isothermal expansion or phase changes, $\Delta T = 0$ while $Q \ne 0$; in adiabatic work, $Q = 0$ while $\Delta T \ne 0$ **[S35]**. | **Diagnostic:** Ask: “Is energy crossing the boundary due to $\Delta T$ ($Q$), due to force$\times$displacement ($W$), or stored inside ($U$)?” <br> **Fix:** Always write $\Delta U = Q - W_{\mathrm{by}}$. Only for an ideal gas does $\Delta T = 0 \iff \Delta U = 0$ (which gives $Q = W_{\mathrm{by}}$, NOT $Q = 0$!). |
| **`ERR-02`: “Cold Flows” & Thermal Sensation / Effusivity Myth** <br> (§`sec:03`, §`sec:10`) | $\bullet$ Saying “cold flows from the ice into the water.” <br> $\bullet$ Claiming a metal doorknob in a $20 °C$ room is at a lower temperature than a wooden door. | Human skin does not measure temperature $T$; it senses transient heat flux $q'' \propto \sqrt{k\rho c_p}\,(T_{\mathrm{skin}} - T_{\mathrm{obj}})$ governed by **thermal effusivity** $e = \sqrt{k\rho c_p}$ **[S12]**. | **Diagnostic:** Are both objects in thermal equilibrium with the same room? <br> **Fix:** By the Zeroth Law, $T_{\mathrm{metal}} = T_{\mathrm{wood}} = T_{\mathrm{room}} = 20 °C$. Heat $\vec{q} = -k\nabla T$ **always** flows from higher $T$ to lower $T$ (from your $33 °C$ fingers into the $20 °C$ metal faster because $e_{\mathrm{metal}} \gg e_{\mathrm{wood}}$). |
| **`ERR-03`: Temperature Additivity & Point vs. Interval ($\Delta T$) Trap** <br> (§`sec:02`, §`sec:03`) | $\bullet$ Converting a temperature *difference* $\Delta T = 20 °C$ to Kelvin as $20 + 273.15 = 293.15 K$! <br> $\bullet$ Converting $\Delta T = 20 °C$ to $°F$ as $1.8(20) + 32 = 68 °F$. <br> $\bullet$ Adding $T_1 + T_2$ of two merged identical cups. | Failing to distinguish an *affine scale point* ($T_K = T_C + 273.15$, $T_F = \frac{9}{5}T_C + 32$) from a *linear difference interval* ($\Delta T_K = T_{K,2} - T_{K,1} = T_{C,2} - T_{C,1} = \Delta T_C$), where additive zero-offsets cancel! | **Diagnostic:** Does the formula have $\Delta T = T_2 - T_1$ (like $mc\Delta T$ or $kA\Delta T/L$) or an absolute $T$? <br> **Fix:** For **intervals** $\Delta T$: <br> $\boxed{\Delta T_K = \Delta T_C = \frac{5}{9}\Delta T_F = \frac{5}{9}\Delta T_R}$ <br> (**Never** add $273.15$ or $32$ to a $\Delta T$ or to a specific heat unit $J/kg/°C \equiv J/kg/K$!). Temperature is intensive: merging two $25 °C$ cups gives $25 °C$, not $50 °C$. |
| **`ERR-04`: Celsius in Absolute-$T$ Ratios, $PV=nRT$, Carnot & $T^4$ Radiation** <br> (§`sec:06`–§`sec:12`) | $\bullet$ Computing $\eta_C = 1 - \frac{27\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}}{227\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}} = 88.1%$ (true value: $40.0%$!). <br> $\bullet$ Using $T$ in $°C$ in $PV=nRT$, $\Delta S = mc\ln(T_2/T_1)$, or $v_{\mathrm{rms}}$. <br> $\bullet$ Replacing $T_1^4 - T_2^4$ with $(T_1 - T_2)^4$ in Stefan–Boltzmann radiation! | Celsius and Fahrenheit have arbitrary non-zero origins ($0 °C = 273.15 K$), so $T_C \le 0$ gives zero, negative, or infinite pressures and efficiencies. Also, $(a - b)^4 \ne a^4 - b^4$. | **Diagnostic:** Circle every standalone $T$, $T_2/T_1$, $T^4$, or $1/T$ in your equation. <br> **Fix:** Convert **every** non-difference temperature to absolute **Kelvin** ($T_K = T_C + 273.15$) or Rankine ($T_R = T_F + 459.67$) *before* writing ratios, logs, roots, or fourth powers. In radiation, compute $T_1^4$ and $T_2^4$ separately in Kelvin: $T_1^4 - T_2^4 = (T_1^2 + T_2^2)(T_1 + T_2)(T_1 - T_2)$. |
| **`ERR-05`: Latent-Heat Plateau Bypass, Missing Subcooled Ice Warming, or Wrong $c_{\mathrm{ice}}$** <br> (§`sec:04`, §`sec:05`) | $\bullet$ Setting $m_i c_i(0 - T_i) + m_i L_f + m_i c_w(T_f - 0) = m_w c_w(T_w - T_f)$ blindly and solving to get $T_f = -19.9 °C$! <br> $\bullet$ Forgetting $m_i c_{\mathrm{ice}}(0 - T_i)$ for ice starting at $-20 °C$, or using $c = 4.184 J/g/K$ for ice! | Assuming all ice melts (or all steam condenses) without checking whether available heat $Q_{\mathrm{avail}}$ is sufficient to cross the $0.0 °C$ fusion plateau ($m_i L_f$) or $100.0 °C$ boiling plateau ($m_s L_v$). | **Diagnostic:** Did your single-equation solve yield $T_f < 0 °C$ when assuming liquid water, or $T_f > 100 °C$? <br> **Fix:** Always run the **3-Gate Inequality Check** (§`sec:04`, Fig. `fig:04:ice-water-tree`) first: compare $Q_{\mathrm{avail}}$ to cool hot water to $0 °C$ against $Q_{\mathrm{warm,ice}} = m_i c_{\mathrm{ice}}(0 - T_i)$ ($\text{use } c_{\mathrm{ice}} = 2.10 J/g/K$) and $Q_{\mathrm{melt}} = m_i L_f$ ($L_f = 333.55 J/g$). If $Q_{\mathrm{warm,ice}} \le Q_{\mathrm{avail}} \le Q_{\mathrm{warm,ice}} + Q_{\mathrm{melt}}$, **stop**: $\mathbf{T_f = 0.00 °C}$ and $m_{\mathrm{melted}} = \dfrac{Q_{\mathrm{avail}} - Q_{\mathrm{warm,ice}}}{L_f}$! |
| **`ERR-06`: Calorimeter Sign Flip, Missing $C_{\mathrm{cal}}$, & Bomb ($\Delta U$) vs. Coffee-Cup ($\Delta H$)** <br> (§`sec:04`, §`sec:07`, §`sec:08`) | $\bullet$ Reporting $\Delta H_{\mathrm{rxn}} = +50 kJ/mol$ (endothermic) when water temperature *rose*! <br> $\bullet$ Omitting calorimeter cup heat capacity $C_{\mathrm{cal}}\Delta T$. <br> $\bullet$ Equating bomb-calorimeter $q_V$ directly to $\Delta H_{\mathrm{rxn}}$ when $\Delta n_{\mathrm{gas}} \ne 0$. | Writing $q_{\mathrm{rxn}} = q_{\mathrm{water}}$ instead of $q_{\mathrm{rxn}} + q_{\mathrm{water}} + q_{\mathrm{cal}} = 0$. Also, a rigid bomb calorimeter measures constant-volume $q_V = \Delta U$, whereas an isobaric coffee cup measures $q_P = \Delta H$. | **Diagnostic:** If $T_{\mathrm{water}}$ rises ($\Delta T_w > 0$), the reaction released heat into the water, so the reaction itself is **exothermic** ($\Delta H_{\mathrm{rxn}} < 0$)! <br> **Fix:** Always use the zero-sum algebraic form: <br> $\boxed{q_{\mathrm{rxn}} = -\left(m_w c_w + C_{\mathrm{cal}}\right)(T_f - T_i)}$ <br> For a rigid bomb calorimeter ($\Delta V = 0$), $\Delta U_{\mathrm{rxn},m} = \dfrac{q_{\mathrm{rxn}}}{n_{\mathrm{rxn}}}$, and convert to enthalpy via $\boxed{\Delta H_{\mathrm{rxn},m} = \Delta U_{\mathrm{rxn},m} + \Delta n_{\mathrm{gas}} R T}$! |

- **Compression ($\Delta V < 0$):** Outside world pushes energy **INTO** gas ($\Delta U$ tends to **increase**): $W_{\mathrm{by}} < 0$ ($w_{\mathrm{on}} = -W_{\mathrm{by}} > 0$). \item **Expansion ($\Delta V > 0$):** Gas spends energy pushing world **OUT** ($\Delta U$ tends to **decrease**): $W_{\mathrm{by}} > 0$ ($w_{\mathrm{on}} < 0$).

| **`ERR-09`: Confusing $C_P$ vs. $C_V$, Molar $C_m$ vs. Specific $c$, & Quantum Freeze-Out** <br> (§`sec:04`, §`sec:06`, §`sec:07`) | $\bullet$ Writing $\Delta U = n C_{P,m}\Delta T$ for an isobaric ideal-gas expansion! <br> $\bullet$ Multiplying mass $m$ $[kg]$ by molar $C_{P,m}$ $[J/mol/K]$. <br> $\bullet$ Using $f = 7$ ($C_{V,m} = \frac{7}{2}R$) for $\mathrm{N_2}$ or $\mathrm{O_2}$ at room temperature $300 K$. | Thinking $\Delta U$ depends on whether the process is isochoric or isobaric. For an ideal gas, $U(T)$ is a function of $T$ **alone**, so $\Delta U = n C_{V,m}\Delta T$ holds for **ALL** processes (isobaric, adiabatic, polytropic)! | **Diagnostic:** For any ideal gas: <br> $\boxed{\Delta U = n C_{V,m}\Delta T \ \ (\text{ALWAYS!})}$ <br> $\boxed{\Delta H = n C_{P,m}\Delta T \ \ (\text{ALWAYS!})}$ <br> whereas $Q_V = \Delta U$ only at constant $V$ and $Q_P = \Delta H$ only at constant $P$! At $300 K$, vibrational modes of light diatomics ($\mathrm{H_2, N_2, O_2, CO}$) are **frozen out** ($k_B T \ll \hbar\omega_{\mathrm{vib}}$), so use $f = 3$ (monatomic, $\gamma = 5/3 = 1.667$), $f = 5$ (diatomic / linear polyatomic at $300 K$, $\gamma = 7/5 = 1.400$), and $f = 6$ (non-linear triatomic at $300 K$, $\gamma = 4/3 = 1.333$). |
| **`ERR-10`: Engine Efficiency $\eta > \eta_C$, Denominator $Q_C$ vs. $Q_H$, & $\mathrm{COP}_R$ vs. $\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP}}$** <br> (§`sec:08`, §`sec:09`) | $\bullet$ Computing $\eta = W_{\mathrm{net}}/Q_C$ instead of $W_{\mathrm{net}}/Q_H = 1 - Q_C/Q_H$. <br> $\bullet$ Using $\mathrm{COP}_R = \frac{T_C}{T_H - T_C}$ for a *heat pump* warming a house, or thinking $\mathrm{COP} = 4.5 > 1$ violates the First Law! | Efficiency / COP is always $\frac{\text{What You Want (Benefit)}}{\text{What You Pay For (Work/Fuel Cost)}}$. For a refrigerator, the benefit is cooling $Q_C$; for a heat pump, the benefit is heating $Q_H = Q_C + W_{\mathrm{in}}$. | **Diagnostic:** Check the identity: <br> $\boxed{\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP}} = \frac{Q_H}{W_{\mathrm{in}}} = \mathrm{COP}_R + 1 > 1}$ <br> A heat pump's $\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP}}$ is **always** $> 1$ (since $Q_H = Q_C + W_{\mathrm{in}}$); it does not create energy — it uses $1\text{ J}$ of work to *move* $\mathrm{COP}_R\text{ J}$ of heat from outdoors plus dump that $1\text{ J}$ indoors! |
| **`ERR-11`: Entropy “Disorder” Myths, $\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}} < 0$, & Irreversible $\Delta S \ne Q/T$** <br> (§`sec:08`) | $\bullet$ Claiming water freezing into ice ($\Delta S_{\mathrm{water}} < 0$) violates the Second Law! <br> $\bullet$ Writing $\Delta S = \int \frac{\delta Q}{T} = 0$ for adiabatic free expansion into vacuum (or irreversible adiabatic throttling / friction). | The Second Law ($\Delta S_{\mathrm{univ}} = \Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}} + \Delta S_{\mathrm{surr}} = S_{\mathrm{gen}} \ge 0$) requires the entropy of the **universe (isolated composite)** to increase, NOT a non-isolated subsystem that exports heat $Q < 0$! Also, $dS = \frac{\delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}}}{T}$ holds **only along a reversible path**. | **Diagnostic:** Is the system isolated, and is the path reversible? <br> **Fix:** Because entropy $S$ is a **state function**, compute $\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}}$ between states $(T_1, V_1)$ and $(T_2, V_2)$ by replacing the irreversible path with ANY convenient **reversible path** connecting the same endpoints: <br> $\boxed{\Delta S_{\mathrm{IG}} = n C_{V,m}\ln\!\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) + nR\ln\!\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right)}$ <br> For adiabatic free expansion ($Q=0, W=0, \Delta T=0$), $\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}} = +nR\ln(V_2/V_1) > 0$ and $\Delta S_{\mathrm{surr}} = 0 \implies S_{\mathrm{gen}} = +nR\ln(V_2/V_1) > 0$! |

### Worked Example — Case Study 1 (`ERR-05`): The Ice–Water Two-Phase Plateau Bypass Trap `[`Cornell E1 Q30d, GGSO E4 Q9a`  **[S25,S28]**]`

In an insulated calorimeter of negligible heat capacity, $m_{\mathrm{ice}} = 80.0 g$ of subcooled ice at $T_{\mathrm{ice}} = -15.0 °C$ ($c_{\mathrm{ice}} = 2.10 J/g/K$, $L_f = 333.55 J/g$) is mixed with $m_w = 120.0 g$ of liquid water at $T_w = 25.0 °C$ ($c_w = 4.184 J/g/K$). Find the final equilibrium temperature $T_f$ and phase composition.
**✗ Naive Trap Path (What $65\%$ of Contestants Write):** Assuming all ice melts to liquid at $T_f > 0 °C$ and solving a single linear equation:
$$m_{\mathrm{ice}}c_{\mathrm{ice}}(0 - (-15.0)) + m_{\mathrm{ice}}L_f + m_{\mathrm{ice}}c_w(T_f - 0) = m_w c_w(25.0 - T_f) \implies 2520.0 + 26684.0 + 334.72\,T_f = 12552.0 - 502.08\,T_f$$
$$836.80\,T_f = -16652.0 \implies T_{f,\mathrm{naive}} = \mathbf{-19.90 °C} \quad (\text{**PHYSICALLY IMPOSSIBLE:** colder than the initial }-15.0 °C\text{ ice!})$$
**✓ Diagnostic & Correct 3-Gate Energy-Budget Solution:**
Why did the naive equation yield $-19.90 °C$? Because it subtracted the full latent heat $26684 J$ of all $80.0 g$ of ice even though the warm water did not have enough thermal energy to melt all the ice! Running the 3-Gate Inequality Check (§`sec:04`):
- **Maximum heat available from cooling $120.0 g$ liquid water to $0.00 °C$:** $Q_{\mathrm{avail}} = m_w c_w(25.0 - 0.0) = (120.0)(4.184)(25.0) = \mathbf{12552.0 J}$.
- **Heat required to warm $80.0 g$ ice from $-15.0 °C$ to $0.00 °C$:** $Q_{\mathrm{warm,ice}} = m_{\mathrm{ice}}c_{\mathrm{ice}}(0.0 - (-15.0)) = (80.0)(2.10)(15.0) = \mathbf{2520.0 J}$.
- **Heat required to melt all $80.0 g$ ice at $0.00 °C$:** $Q_{\mathrm{melt,all}} = m_{\mathrm{ice}}L_f = (80.0)(333.55) = \mathbf{26684.0 J}$.

Since $Q_{\mathrm{warm,ice}} = 2520.0 J < Q_{\mathrm{avail}} = 12552.0 J < Q_{\mathrm{warm,ice}} + Q_{\mathrm{melt,all}} = 29204.0 J$, the system arrests on the **$0.00 °C$ two-phase fusion plateau**:
$$\boxed{T_f = \mathbf{0.00 °C}\ (273.15 K)}, \qquad m_{\mathrm{melted}} = \frac{Q_{\mathrm{avail}} - Q_{\mathrm{warm,ice}}}{L_f} = \frac{12552.0 - 2520.0}{333.55} = \frac{10032.0 J}{333.55 J/g} = \mathbf{30.08 g}$$
Final equilibrium composition at $0.00 °C$: $120.0 + 30.08 = \mathbf{150.08 g\text{**liquid water**}}$ and $80.0 - 30.08 = \mathbf{49.92 g\text{**solid ice**}}$.

**✓ Verified by:** Enthalpy conservation relative to $0 °C$ ice reference state and Python check `sec20:ex20.1`.

- **✗ Wrong Move (i) — Factoring $(T_1 - T_2)^4$:** $\sigma(100.0)^4 = \mathbf{5.67 W}$ (underestimates true heat loss by $\mathbf{175\times}$!).
- **✗ Wrong Move (ii) — Fourth Powers in Celsius:** $\sigma(127.0^4 - 27.0^4) = \mathbf{14.72 W}$ (underestimates true heat loss by $\mathbf{67.5\times}$!).

### Worked Example — Case Study 4 (`ERR-07`, `ERR-08`, `ERR-09`): Isobaric Compression Work Sign, $C_P$ vs. $C_V$ & Gauge Pressure `[Rules 5.c.ii, 5.c.iv]`

A cylinder fitted with a weighted piston at constant **gauge pressure** $P_{\mathrm{gauge}} = 150.0 kPa$ (in standard atmospheric pressure $P_{\mathrm{atm}} = 101.325 kPa$) contains $n = 0.800 mol$ of diatomic nitrogen ($\mathrm{N_2}$, $f = 5$ at $300 K$). The gas is cooled isobarically so its volume contracts from $V_1 = 12.00 L$ to $V_2 = 8.00 L$. Find: (a) the work $W_{\mathrm{by}}$ done *by* the gas and work $w_{\mathrm{on}}$ done *on* the gas, (b) the internal energy change $\Delta U$, and (c) the heat $Q$ transferred into the gas.
**Solution.**
(a) **Trap 1 (`ERR-07` Gauge vs. Absolute Pressure):** Using $P_{\mathrm{gauge}} = 150.0 kPa$ gives $|W| = 600.0 J$ (wrong by $405.3 J$!). True absolute pressure is:
$$P_{\mathrm{abs}} = P_{\mathrm{gauge}} + P_{\mathrm{atm}} = 150.0 + 101.325 = \mathbf{251.325 kPa}$$
**Trap 2 (`ERR-08` Work Sign on Compression $\Delta V = -4.00 L < 0$):** Because the gas contracts, work done *by* the gas is **negative**:
$$W_{\mathrm{by}} = P_{\mathrm{abs}}(V_2 - V_1) = (251.325 kPa)(8.00 L - 12.00 L) = \mathbf{-1005.3 J}, \qquad w_{\mathrm{on}} = -W_{\mathrm{by}} = \mathbf{+1005.3 J}$$
(b) **Trap 3 (`ERR-09` Using $C_P$ instead of $C_V$ for $\Delta U$ in an Isobaric Process):** Even though pressure is constant, internal energy change of an ideal gas is **always** $\Delta U = n C_{V,m}\Delta T = \frac{f}{2}nR\Delta T = \frac{5}{2}P_{\mathrm{abs}}(V_2 - V_1)$:
$$\Delta U = \frac{5}{2}(-1005.3 J) = \mathbf{-2513.25 J}$$
(c) By the First Law $\Delta U = Q - W_{\mathrm{by}}$ (or $Q_P = \Delta H = n C_{P,m}\Delta T = \frac{7}{2}P_{\mathrm{abs}}\Delta V$):
$$Q = \Delta U + W_{\mathrm{by}} = -2513.25 J + (-1005.30 J) = \mathbf{-3518.55 J} \quad (3518.55 J\text{ of heat flows **out** of the gas})$$

**✓ Verified by:** Identity $Q_P / \Delta U = C_P / C_V = \gamma = 7/5 = (-3518.55)/(-2513.25) = 1.4000$ and Python check `sec20:ex20.4`.

### Worked Example — Case Study 5 (`ERR-06`, `ERR-11`): Bomb Calorimeter $\Delta U \to \Delta H$ & Freezing Water Entropy $\Delta S_{\mathrm{univ}} > 0$ `[Rules 5.c.iv, 5.c.vii]`

(a) Combustion of $n_{\mathrm{sample}} = 0.0250 mol$ of liquid benzene ($\mathrm{C_6H_6(\ell)} + \frac{15}{2}\mathrm{O_2(g)} \to 6\mathrm{CO_2(g)} + 3\mathrm{H_2O(\ell)}$) in a rigid constant-volume bomb calorimeter at $T = 298.15 K$ warms the calorimeter bath ($C_{\mathrm{tot}} = m_w c_w + C_{\mathrm{bomb}} = 10.500 kJ/K$) by $\Delta T = +7.780 K$. Compute the molar internal energy of combustion $\Delta U_{c,m}^\circ$ and molar enthalpy of combustion $\Delta H_{c,m}^\circ$.
(b) When $m = 1.000 kg$ of supercooled liquid water freezes into ice at $T_m = 0.00 °C = 273.15 K$ inside a freezer room held at $T_{\mathrm{surr}} = -10.00 °C = 263.15 K$ (releasing $Q_{\mathrm{out}} = m L_f = 333.55 kJ$ to the freezer room), calculate $\Delta S_{\mathrm{water}}$, $\Delta S_{\mathrm{surr}}$, and $\Delta S_{\mathrm{univ}}$ to prove why freezing does not violate the Second Law.
**Solution.**
(a) **Sign & Bomb Volume Correction (`ERR-06`):** Because the bath warmed ($\Delta T > 0$), heat left the reaction: $q_V = -C_{\mathrm{tot}}\Delta T = -(10.500 kJ/K)(7.780 K) = -81.690 kJ$. In a rigid bomb ($\Delta V = 0$), this equals $\Delta U$, so per mole of benzene:
$$\Delta U_{c,m}^\circ = \frac{q_V}{n_{\mathrm{sample}}} = \frac{-81.690 kJ}{0.0250 mol} = \mathbf{-3267.6 kJ/mol}$$
From the balanced stoichiometry, gaseous moles change by $\Delta n_{\mathrm{gas}} = n_{\mathrm{gas,prod}} - n_{\mathrm{gas,react}} = 6 - 7.5 = \mathbf{-1.500\,mol gas per mole C_6H_6}$. Thus:
$$\Delta H_{c,m}^\circ = \Delta U_{c,m}^\circ + \Delta n_{\mathrm{gas}} R T = -3267.60 kJ/mol + (-1.500)(8.31446×10^{-3} kJ/mol/K)(298.15 K) = -3267.60 - 3.718 = \mathbf{-3271.32 kJ/mol}$$
(b) **Second-Law Entropy Ledger (`ERR-11`):** At the reversible phase-change temperature $T_m = 273.15 K$, the freezing water loses heat $Q_{\mathrm{sys}} = -333550 J$, while the $263.15 K$ freezer air absorbs $Q_{\mathrm{surr}} = +333550 J$:
$$\Delta S_{\mathrm{water}} = \frac{Q_{\mathrm{sys}}}{T_m} = \frac{-333550 J}{273.15 K} = \mathbf{-1221.12 J/K} < 0, \qquad \Delta S_{\mathrm{surr}} = \frac{Q_{\mathrm{surr}}}{T_{\mathrm{surr}}} = \frac{+333550 J}{263.15 K} = \mathbf{+1267.53 J/K} > 0$$
$$\Delta S_{\mathrm{univ}} = \Delta S_{\mathrm{water}} + \Delta S_{\mathrm{surr}} = -1221.12 + 1267.53 = \mathbf{+46.40 J/K} > 0 \quad (\text{Second Law satisfied!})$$

**✓ Verified by:** Direct identity $\Delta S_{\mathrm{univ}} = Q_{\mathrm{out}}(1/T_{\mathrm{surr}} - 1/T_m) = 333550(10.00)/(263.15\times 273.15) = +46.404 J/K$ and Python check `sec20:ex20.5`.

### Worked Example — Case Study 6 (`ERR-10`): Two-Law Filter for Inventor Engine & Heat-Pump Claims `[Rules 5.c.iv, 5.c.vii]`

Three inventors propose cyclic devices operating between a hot reservoir at $T_H = 600.0 K$ and a cold reservoir at $T_C = 300.0 K$:
(1) *Engine A* absorbs $Q_H = 1000 J$, produces work $W = 450 J$, and rejects $Q_C = 500 J$.
(2) *Engine B* absorbs $Q_H = 1000 J$, produces work $W = 550 J$, and rejects $Q_C = 450 J$.
(3) *Heat Pump C* consumes work $W_{\mathrm{in}} = 250 J$ to deliver $Q_H = 450 J$ of heat to the $600.0 K$ reservoir while extracting $Q_C = 200 J$ from $300.0 K$.
Audit each claim against **both** the First Law and the Second Law (Clausius inequality $\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} \le 0$ / Carnot limit).
**Solution.**
The Carnot efficiency bound is $\eta_C = 1 - \frac{300.0}{600.0} = \mathbf{0.5000}$ ($50.0%$) and the Carnot heat-pump bound is $\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP,C}} = \frac{T_H}{T_H - T_C} = \frac{600.0}{300.0} = \mathbf{2.000}$:
- **Engine A:** $\eta_A = \frac{450}{1000} = 0.450 < \eta_C = 0.500$ (passes naive Carnot check!), **TRAP:** Check the First Law $Q_H \stackrel{?}{=} W + Q_C$: $1000 \ne 450 + 500 = 950\,J$ ($50 J$ of energy disappears per cycle!). **Verdict: Impossible — violates the First Law!**
- **Engine B:** First Law holds ($Q_H = 550 + 450 = 1000 J$), **TRAP:** $\eta_B = \frac{550}{1000} = \mathbf{0.550} > \eta_C = 0.500$ (entropy generated per cycle is $S_{\mathrm{gen}} = \frac{Q_C}{T_C} - \frac{Q_H}{T_H} = \frac{450}{300} - \frac{1000}{600} = 1.500 - 1.667 = -0.167 J/K < 0$). **Verdict: Impossible — violates the Second Law!**
- **Heat Pump C:** First Law holds ($Q_H = Q_C + W_{\mathrm{in}} = 200 + 250 = 450 J$), and actual $\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP}} = \frac{Q_H}{W_{\mathrm{in}}} = \frac{450}{250} = \mathbf{1.800} \le \mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP,C}} = 2.000$ (with $S_{\mathrm{gen}} = \frac{450}{600} - \frac{200}{300} = 0.750 - 0.667 = \mathbf{+0.0833 J/K} > 0$). *(Notice that if a student mistakenly compared $\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP}} = 1.800$ against the refrigerator Carnot bound $\mathrm{COP}_{R,C} = \frac{300}{300} = 1.000$, they would falsely reject Heat Pump C!).* **Verdict: Physically feasible irreversible heat pump!**

**✓ Verified by:** First-Law conservation $\Delta U_{\mathrm{cycle}} = 0$ and Clausius integral $S_{\mathrm{gen}} = Q_H/T_H - Q_C/T_C \ge 0$ in Python check `sec20:ex20.6`.

### Worked Example — Case Study 7 (`ERR-14`, `ERR-15`): Critical Radius Paradox ($r_{\mathrm{crit}} = k/h$) vs. 2027 Cup & Parasitic Thermal Mass `[Rules 3.a–e, 5.c.iii  **[S12]**]`

  $$R'_{\mathrm{ins,wire}} = \frac{\ln(4.00/1.00)}{2\pi(0.0400)} + \frac{1}{2\pi(0.00400)(10.0)} = 5.5159 + 3.9789 = \mathbf{9.4948 m · K/W} < R'_{\mathrm{bare,wire}}\text{!}$$

- **2027 Competition Cup ($r_i = 34.0 mm \gg r_{\mathrm{crit}} = 4.00 mm \to r_o = 37.0 mm$):**

  $$R'_{\mathrm{ins,cup}} = \frac{\ln(37.0/34.0)}{2\pi(0.0400)} + \frac{1}{2\pi(0.0370)(10.0)} = 0.33648 + 0.43015 = \mathbf{0.76663 m · K/W} > R'_{\mathrm{bare,cup}}\text{!}$$
  Heat loss **DECREASES** by $1 - \frac{0.46810}{0.76663} = \mathbf{-38.94\%}$ because $r_i \gg r_{\mathrm{crit}}$!

(b) **Parasitic Thermal Mass Drain (`ERR-15`):** Water heat capacity is $C_w = (0.1000)(4184) = 418.4 J/K$, while the heavy aluminum sleeve has $C_{\mathrm{Al}} = (0.1400)(900.0) = 126.0 J/K$ ($30.1\%$ of the water!). Without the heavy sleeve, $\mathrm{TG}_{\mathrm{ideal}} = \frac{9200 J}{418.4 J/K} = \mathbf{21.99 °C}$; with the coupled sleeve, $\mathrm{TG}_{\mathrm{with\text{ }Al}} = \frac{9200 J}{418.4 + 126.0} = \mathbf{16.90 °C}$ — the heavy absorber robs $\mathbf{5.09 °C}$ ($\mathbf{23.1\%}$) of temperature gain!

| 3. *“Because $Q = 0$ in adiabatic free expansion into a vacuum, the entropy change $\Delta S$ of the gas is zero.”* | **FALSE** | Free expansion is **irreversible** ($\Delta S > \int \delta Q/T = 0$). Since $S$ is a state function, evaluating along a reversible isotherm gives $\Delta S = +nR\ln(V_2/V_1) > 0$. |
| 4. *“The Second Law of Thermodynamics forbids the entropy of any system from decreasing ($\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}} < 0$).”* | **FALSE** | Any non-isolated system that transfers heat out ($Q < 0$, e.g., cooling coffee or freezing water) has $\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}} < 0$, provided $\Delta S_{\mathrm{surr}} \ge \|\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}}\|$ so $\Delta S_{\mathrm{univ}} \ge 0$. |
| 5. *“For an ideal gas undergoing a constant-pressure (isobaric) expansion, $\Delta U = n C_{P,m}\Delta T$.”* | **FALSE** | For an ideal gas, internal energy depends ONLY on $T$, so $\Delta U = n C_{V,m}\Delta T$ for **all** processes! At constant pressure, $Q_P = \Delta H = n C_{P,m}\Delta T$ and $\Delta U = Q_P - P\Delta V = n C_{V,m}\Delta T$. |
| 6. *“A heat pump with a Coefficient of Performance $\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP}} = 4.2$ violates conservation of energy because efficiency cannot exceed $100\%$.”* | **FALSE** | Heat engine efficiency $\eta = W/Q_H \le 1 - T_C/T_H < 1$, whereas a heat pump moves ambient heat: $\mathrm{COP}_{\mathrm{HP}} = Q_H/W_{\mathrm{in}} = 1 + Q_C/W_{\mathrm{in}} \ge 1$ (Carnot limit $T_H/(T_H - T_C)$ can easily exceed $4$!). |
| 7. *“At the same temperature $T$, all gas molecules have the same rms speed $v_{\mathrm{rms}}$.”* | **FALSE** | By equipartition, all gases at $T$ have the same mean translational *kinetic energy* $\bar{K}_{\mathrm{tr}} = \frac{3}{2}k_B T$, so lighter molecules move faster: $v_{\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{3RT/M} \propto M^{-1/2}$ ($\mathrm{H_2}$ moves $3.74\times$ faster than $\mathrm{N_2}$). |
| 8. *“Raising the pressure on any solid always raises its melting point temperature.”* | **FALSE** | By Clapeyron $\frac{dP}{dT} = \frac{L_f}{T(v_\ell - v_s)}$: because ordinary ice ($\mathrm{Ice\ Ih}$), bismuth, and gallium contract upon melting ($v_\ell < v_s$), increasing pressure **lowers** their melting point ($\frac{dP}{dT} < 0$). |
| 9. *“Passing a real gas or ideal gas through an insulated throttling valve ($h_2 = h_1$) always cools the gas.”* | **FALSE** | An ideal gas has $h = h(T) \implies \Delta T = 0$ in throttling ($\mu_{\mathrm{JT}} = 0$). A real gas cools upon throttling ($\mu_{\mathrm{JT}} = (\partial T/\partial P)_H > 0$) **only** below its Joule–Thomson inversion temperature $T_{\mathrm{inv}}$ ($\mathrm{He}$ and $\mathrm{H_2}$ warm at $300 K$!). |
| 10. *“A good reflector of visible light (such as white paint or snow) is also a good reflector of room-temperature thermal infrared radiation.”* | **FALSE** | White paint and snow reflect visible solar radiation ($\alpha_{\mathrm{vis}} \approx 0.15\text{--}0.26$), but at room-temperature mid-IR ($\lambda \approx 10 µm$) both have $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{MIR}} \approx 0.90\text{--}0.98$ (nearly black!). |
| 11. *“Adding insulation to any pipe or wire always decreases the rate of heat loss.”* | **FALSE** | If bare cylinder radius $r_i < r_{\mathrm{crit}} = k_{\mathrm{ins}}/h_{\mathrm{ext}}$ (or sphere $r_i < 2k/h$), adding insulation **increases** heat loss until outer radius reaches $r_o = r_{\mathrm{crit}}$ (Ex. `ex:20.7`). |
| 12. *“The Biot number $\mathrm{Bi} = h L_c / k$ and Nusselt number $\mathrm{Nu} = h L / k$ are identical dimensionless numbers.”* | **FALSE** | $\mathrm{Bi} = h L_c / k_{\mathrm{solid}}$ uses the **solid body's** conductivity (testing internal temperature uniformity $\mathrm{Bi}<0.1$), whereas $\mathrm{Nu} = h L / k_{\mathrm{fluid}}$ uses the **moving fluid's** conductivity! |
